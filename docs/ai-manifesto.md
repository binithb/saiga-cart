# Agile in the AI Age

AI can make coding and exploring designs faster. It cannot, on its own, establish
what customers need or which problems are worth solving. Faster implementation
can carry a mistaken assumption further before anyone questions it.

*If the foundation is crooked, everything built on it will be crooked.*

Our foundation is shared understanding - not a fixed specification. We strengthen
it through conversation, small experiments, and feedback.

Building on Agile, we give priority to:

**Shared understanding over individual prompting speed**

**Useful outcomes over implementation speed**

**Customer discovery over detailed instructions**

**Learning and adaptation over uninterrupted execution**

Speed, instructions, and focused execution remain valuable. They serve
understanding and useful delivery, rather than substitute for them.

## Twelve principles

1. Understand the customer's problem, and deliver small, useful improvements that let us discover whether our understanding is right.
2. Welcome changing requirements as new learning, and update shared intent before continuing work affected by the change.
3. Deliver frequently in small increments, using prototypes and working software to test assumptions before expanding implementation.
4. Bring customers, domain experts, and developers together to refine needs; use AI to explore questions, examples, and alternatives.
5. Trust people to lead and own consequential decisions; give AI clear boundaries and autonomy proportionate to risk and reversibility.
6. Use conversation to resolve uncertainty, then preserve important conclusions, assumptions, and reasons where people and agents can find them.
7. Measure progress by useful working software; passing tests show that specified behavior works, not that we chose the right problem.
8. Keep generation within the team's capacity to understand, review, and maintain the result; faster output must not create unsustainable work.
9. Maintain clear designs, appropriate tests, and human review; neither polished specifications nor plausible code remove the need for engineering judgment.
10. Build and document only what the next useful step requires, with effort proportionate to uncertainty, scope, and risk.
11. Let teams shape and evolve their designs, keeping decisions available to others and revisiting them when evidence changes.
12. Reflect on misunderstood needs as well as implementation failures, improving how the team discovers, communicates, builds, and learns.

## What changes in practice

Agile already values customers, collaboration, working software, and change.
AI makes shortcuts around these values more costly - not the values themselves
obsolete.

| Familiar Agile theme | AI-era anti-pattern | Practical adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People and interactions | Each person works quickly with AI, but their understanding of the problem diverges. | Agree on the intended outcome together. Preserve important context so the next person or agent does not have to guess. |
| Working software | Generated code meets the written criteria, but the criteria describe the wrong solution. | Check both whether the software behaves as intended and whether that behavior helps the customer. |
| Customer collaboration | A well-written story or detailed prompt is mistaken for a well-understood need. | Refine stories through questions, concrete examples, and customer feedback. Make important assumptions visible rather than presenting guesses as facts. |
| Responding to change | AI continues implementing a plan after new evidence has undermined its assumptions. | Pause affected work, revise the shared understanding, and take the next small step in the new direction. |

Requirements need not be complete before coding begins. Understand enough to
choose a bounded next step, make uncertainty explicit, and use the result to learn.

AI can help identify ambiguity, suggest questions, draft stories, and build
experiments. People remain responsible for engaging customers, choosing
priorities, and accepting trade-offs. Clear instructions help us build correctly;
feedback helps us discover whether we are building the right thing.

Keep shared context short and current: what is true, what changes next, why, and
how the result will be evaluated. A local edit needs little ceremony; broader
delegation needs clearer boundaries and stronger evidence.
